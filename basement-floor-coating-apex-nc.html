Why prep matters

Moisture-aware prep for NC basements

Basement slabs often deal with humidity and vapor drive that garage slabs don't. Skipping moisture checks or grinding is a common reason basement coatings blister or peel.

Independent local contractors mechanically grind the slab, assess moisture, repair cracks and pits, and only then apply a system suited to below-grade concrete — so the finish bonds and holds up.

  • Diamond grinding for a real mechanical bond
  • Moisture-aware system selection for basements
  • Crack, pit, and joint repair before coating
  • Solid-color, flake, or metallic finishes
  • Clear walkthrough of finish and cure times up front

Common questions

Basement floor coating FAQs

Can you coat a basement floor in Apex?

Yes. Basements are one of the most common concrete coating projects — a sealed floor cuts dust, resists stains, and makes the space far more usable. A contractor will check for moisture and recommend the right system.

How much does basement floor coating cost?

Coatings typically run about $3–$7 per square foot depending on slab condition, moisture mitigation needs, and the finish you choose. Metallic and multi-coat flake systems sit toward the higher end. The best number comes from a free on-site estimate.

Will it handle humidity and moisture?

Below-grade slabs need moisture-aware prep and the right coating system. Independent contractors assess vapor drive and humidity during the estimate and choose a finish suited to basement conditions.

How long before I can use the basement again?

Most residential basement jobs are one to two days. Polyaspartic top coats often handle foot traffic the next day; traditional epoxy systems need a little more cure time. A contractor will give you exact timing for your finish.
